## What Assessment Does

In the Assessment rotation, you will learn and practice cybersecurity assessments that help clients improve their organizations' security posture. 

Using the NIST Cybersecurity Framework and the CIS Critical Security Controls as examples, students are introduced to commonly used cybersecurity assessment standards. Working with an ORTSOC client, student teams will select a framework appropriate to the client’s cybersecurity goals and conduct an assessment. The results of the assessment will be a written analysis with recommendations to be presented to the client in-person or virtually while addressing the client’s questions and concerns.

**What you will be evaluated on:**

- Monitor and evaluate a client's compliance with industry-standard cybersecurity frameworks  (ex: the Center for Internet Security CIS Controls, the National Institute for Standards and Technology (NIST) Cybersecurity Framework, and NIST publication 800-53, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations).
- Perform security reviews, identify gaps in security architecture, and develop an improvement plan.
- Develop cybersecurity assessment reports.
- Develop project plans.
- Verify implementation of cybersecurity controls.

## Types of Assessments

The Assessment rotation focuses on administrative assessments rather than technical assessments like penetration testing. Some of the assessments that you may conduct during this rotation include:

- **Maturity Assessment**: Evaluation of an organization's administrative and technical security posture compared to an accepted standard to describe the level an organization is prepared to detect, identify, and respond to cyberattacks.
- **Risk Assessment**: Identify likelihood, frequency, and impact on an organization if exposure or vulnerability is exploited.
- **ORTSOC Onboarding Assessment**: Identify information about a client’s network and data architecture for future installation of sensors You may also gather information to form the basis for other assessments.

## NIST Cybersecurity Framework (NIST CSF)

NIST CSF is a set of guidelines we use to evaluate client's security posture. Most government organizations use NIST CSF as a best-practice baseline. The NIST CSF groups guidelines into 6 functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover. By looking through the guidelines of each function's categories and subcategories, we can compare them with clients's current practices in order to put them into a maturity rating.

### NIST CSF Tiers
- Tier 1: Partial Implementation
- Tier 2: Risk Informed Implementation
- Tier 3: Repeatable Processes
- Tier 4: Adaptive

## CIS Controls

CIS Controls are another set of guidelines used to evaluate security posture. There are 18 CIS controls, each with their own safeguards. These controls and safeguards are grouped into 3 implementation groups that are prioritized depending on the size of an organization and their IT and security personnel. ORTSOC tends to lean into CIS Controls are they are prioritized: Implmentation Group 1 safeguards are more important to address than Implementation Group 2, etc.

### CIS Controls Maturity Levels
1. Policy Defined
2. Control Implemented
3. Control Automated or Technically Enforced
4. Control Reported to Business

## Beginner Tips
- Know how CIS Controls map to NIST CSF subcategories. There is a guide provided, but not every safeguard maps 1-to-1.
- Be comfortable about what the different functions of NIST CSF and CIS Controls cover.
- Start working on your report early.
- Keep active communication with the people in your group.
