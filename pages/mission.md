# Mission and How ORTSOC Works

## What ORTSOC is

ORTSOC is a Managed Security Services Provider (MSSP) staffed by students and guided by experienced faculty and staff. It serves a group of regional clients that could not otherwise afford cybersecurity services.

## The "Cybersecurity Teaching Hospital"

Medical and veterinary students do clinical rotations in their 4th year at a teaching hospital. ORTSOC works the same way for cybersecurity:

| Teaching hospital | ORTSOC |
|---|---|
| Patients | Clients (small and mid-size public, not-for-profit, and educational organizations) |
| Clinical rotations | Rotations in security specialties |
| Attending physicians | Faculty, staff, and mentors with years of experience |

## How the year is organized

- The practicum is a three-class series (CS 424/425/426 for undergraduates, CS 524/525/526 for graduate students).
- You complete **six rotations** across the academic year, each about five weeks long.
  - Network Security Monitoring & Incident Response
  - Threat Intelligence
  - Security Engineering
  - Security Assessment
  - Penetration Testing
- You start with an orientation, then apply what you learn as a security analyst on real work.
- Graduate students also do a weekly research seminar, a literature review, and a presentation.

## Ethics and trust

Trust is everything in the security community. ORTSOC follows the ISC2 Code of Ethics:

1. Protect society, the common good, public trust, and infrastructure.
2. Act honorably, honestly, justly, responsibly, and legally.
3. Provide diligent and competent service to principals.
4. Advance and protect the profession.

In practice this means:

- Never use client information to your advantage.
- Never share client information outside ORTSOC, and never store it on personal devices.
- Never post client details, ORTSOC infrastructure, policies, or logs on social media.
- Ask any faculty member if you are unsure whether something is ethical.
